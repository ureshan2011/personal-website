  2. No. 02Journal

    MDPI MTI · 2025

    Evaluating Spatial Decision-Making and Player Experience in a Remote Multiplayer Augmented Reality Hide-and-Seek Game

    Study 0260 participants
    yasassri.me/cards02/13
    The finding

    Hide-and-seek between players in different places, compared with playing in the same room. AR changed where people hid and searched: they used mid-air and vertical space. A lightweight view-cone cue made players feel connected without avatars or video.

    Multimodal Technologies and Interaction, 9(8), 79. MDPI. Open access. With H. Lukosch, J. Everett & S. Lukosch.

    ACM OzCHI · 2025

    On the Impact of Augmented Reality Game Mechanics on the Player Experience in Remote Multiplayer Gameplay

    Study 0338 participants
    yasassri.me/cards03/13
    The finding

    Real-time object manipulation, particle-effect feedback and time pressure, built into remote multiplayer AR play. These core AR mechanics deepened strategic search and engagement when they came with clear feedback and well-calibrated constraints.

    37th Australian Conference on Human-Computer Interaction (OzCHI ’25). ACM. With H. Lukosch, J. Everett & S. Lukosch.

  5. No. 05Journal

    Elsevier · 2025

    Representing Remote Locations with Location-Based Augmented Reality Game Design

    Study 0130 participants
    yasassri.me/cards05/13
    The finding

    Three ways to show someone a remote room in AR: Window, Overlay and Tabletop. Overlay gave the strongest sense of “being there”; Window felt like the remote place extending into your own; Tabletop gave a detached, strategic overview.

    Entertainment Computing, 53, 100932. Elsevier. With H. Lukosch, J. Everett & S. Lukosch.

  6. No. 06Thesis

    PhD · University of Canterbury

    Designing Multiplayer Location-Based Augmented Reality Games that Connect Remote Players and Places

    3 studies128 participants
    yasassri.me/cards06/13
    The question

    How can augmented reality make meaningful use of the real world so remote players can play together, live? Three user studies answer it: how to represent a remote space, how players decide where to go in it, and which AR mechanics make distributed play meaningful.

    PhD thesis, Human Interface Technology, HIT Lab NZ, University of Canterbury. Supervised by S. Lukosch, H. Lukosch & J. Everett.
